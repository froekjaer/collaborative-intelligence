Collaborative Intelligence

A Research Program exploring how autonomous AI agents and human operators collaborate in safety-critical industrial systems.

A Research Program

Six Core Principles

Human Accountability

Every autonomous decision is traceable to a human operator. AI proposes; humans approve. Audit logs are non-negotiable.

Explicit Uncertainty

When confidence is low, the system must say so. Silent failures are worse than noisy ones. Uncertainty is a first-class signal.

Empirical Validation

Claims about system behavior must be backed by logs, metrics, or reproducible experiments. No hand-waving allowed.

Multi-Agent Divergence

Different agents can disagree. Divergence is data. The system learns from conflict, not just consensus.

Operational Trust

Trust is earned through consistent behavior in production, not certificates or compliance checklists. Reality beats sandbox.

Continuous Feedback

The loop never closes. Every deployment feeds back into the model. Every incident updates the protocol.

Key Findings

Divergence Protocol

When agents disagree meaningfully, the divergence itself becomes training data — not noise.

Incident Logs > Trust Models

Operational incident logs are more predictive of system trust than static trust models.

Tilretning as Training Data

Human adjustments ("tilretning") during operations are high-value training signals.

Reality Beats Sandbox

Production environments expose edge cases that synthetic testing never catches.
