# Capacity-adapted participation — research track 02

**Status:** Research proposal, 2026-11-15. Not a clinical intervention, legal advice, employment placement programme or evidence of efficacy.  
**Parent:** [Collaborative Intelligence v2 foundations](./COLLABORATIVE_INTELLIGENCE_V2_FOUNDATIONS.md). **Related:** [Neuroinclusion and youth capabilities](./NEUROINCLUSION_YOUTH_CAPABILITIES_01.md).

## Research premise

People of different ages may be unable or unwilling to meet conventional employment schedules because of fluctuating health, chronic pain, fatigue, disability, caregiving, psychological strain, age-related circumstances or other constraints. Some may welcome meaningful participation through flexible, accessible and supported activities. Participation is an **option**, never a condition of dignity, benefits or deserving support.

**Hypothesis, not proven effect:** person-led, capacity-adapted participation may improve belonging, perceived agency, opportunities to contribute and recipient outcomes when compared with inaccessible conventional arrangements. Effects may also be negative (fatigue, symptom exacerbation, guilt, coercion, financial loss or social isolation).

## Distinguish participation from employment

- Paid work, self-employment, volunteering, peer support, mentorship, civic participation, creative practice, learning and simply belonging are different valid forms of social life.
- A person's worth and entitlement to support do not depend on productivity or measurable output.
- Never use participation metrics as a proxy for medical work capacity, employability, benefit eligibility or ability to sustain regular hours.
- Respect local law and individual circumstances. In Denmark, participation and remuneration may affect social benefits and reporting duties; require qualified, case-specific legal guidance before implementing any compensated scheme. Do not assume an arrangement is benefit-neutral.
- Protect rights to compensation, accessible conditions, safe work and non-discrimination; avoid substituting unpaid participation for jobs that should be paid.

## Candidate mechanism: reciprocal, capacity-adapted participation

1. **Person-defined purpose:** The participant chooses whether to engage, what is meaningful, and whether they want compensation, learning, community or a bounded contribution.
2. **Voluntary capacity envelope:** Preferences for duration, timing, intensity, sensory load, communication and recovery are self-defined and changeable; no diagnosis disclosure required by default.
3. **Task/environment adaptation:** Break work into bounded, asynchronous, handoff-friendly contributions with accessibility and recovery; change organisations and tasks, not just people.
4. **AI as optional scaffold:** Provide context summaries, task decomposition, accessibility support and handoff continuity; preserve human review and avoid health inference or monitoring.
5. **Reciprocal value:** Track separately the person's own stated outcomes, benefits to recipients, mentor/organiser burden, remuneration and unintended externalities.
6. **Stop and feedback:** Easy refusal, pauses, withdrawal and no penalty. Real-world health, wellbeing and agency feedback can change the activity or end it.

## Candidate use cases (not prescriptions)

- Short, optional knowledge-sharing or mentorship sessions by experienced specialists.
- Asynchronous review of accessible technical documentation, educational material or community resources.
- Peer support or civic participation with safeguarding and consent.
- Small creative, research or quality-assurance contributions with realistic deadlines and fair compensation where applicable.
- Learning-only participation, without any obligation to produce.

Avoid turning these into presumed roles for a diagnosis, age group or health condition.

## Research questions and comparisons

- **RQ-P1:** Does participant-controlled pacing improve self-defined belonging and autonomy compared with standard scheduled participation?
- **RQ-P2:** Do microtasks and asynchronous handoffs improve sustainable participation **without** increasing symptom burden or organiser workload?
- **RQ-P3:** Does optional AI assistance reduce coordination burden while preserving privacy and independent skills?
- **RQ-P4:** Who receives value and who bears costs? Measure participant, recipient, mentor, organisation and public-system effects separately.
- **RQ-P5:** Which safeguards prevent coercion, unpaid labour substitution, benefit-related harm, stigma and diagnosis-based profiling?
- **RQ-P6:** Does the model remain accessible to people with very limited capacity, including those who choose not to contribute?

**Comparators:** accessible existing peer support, volunteering, supported employment where appropriate, and no-participation-with-full-support. Do not set an artificially weak baseline.  
**Outcomes:** self-reported meaning, autonomy, belonging, fatigue and recovery; participation retention without pressure; participant-defined success; independently assessed recipient benefit; fair remuneration; privacy incidents; opportunity costs; adverse outcomes. Report variation and uncertainty rather than one composite productivity score.

## Safeguards before any pilot

- Co-design with people who have lived experience of fluctuating capacity, disability-rights advocates, clinicians where appropriate, employers, benefit advisers and relevant community organisations.
- Human-subject research requires suitable ethics review, accessible informed consent, data minimisation, safeguarding and independent withdrawal/complaints routes.
- No medical diagnosis collection by default; do not infer capacity from attendance, AI usage, output or availability.
- No mandatory productivity targets, streaks, gamification or constant notifications; account for fatigue and recovery.
- Ensure accessibility, reasonable accommodation, and legal review of pay, insurance, liability, tax, social benefits and volunteer/employment classification before real participation.
- Evaluate whether organisational cost-saving shifts hidden burdens to participants, families or public services.
- Publish negative results and stop if harms outweigh benefits.

## Extension: overloaded employees and voluntary association governance

Capacity adaptation is not limited to people excluded from paid employment. It is equally relevant to **people already working under excessive load** and to **volunteers and board members** whose unpaid responsibilities compete with paid work, family, care and recovery. These are three different institutional settings with distinct legal duties, authority structures, incentives and risks; do not pool outcomes as though they were identical.

**Employment setting:** Study workload, interruption costs, meeting load, task fragmentation, control over schedules, psychosocial safety and actual recovery. AI may reduce administrative burden or may intensify expectations, monitoring and work pace. Measure total human effort, not just time saved on an individual task. Employers retain duties that cannot be delegated to an AI system.

**Volunteer and association setting:** Study role clarity, concentration of invisible work on chairs/secretaries/treasurers, board succession, documentation handover, burnout, unequal access to participation and governance quality. AI may support minutes, briefing, document retrieval, draft preparation and continuity, but accountable board decisions, confidentiality and democratic legitimacy stay with the humans authorised under association rules. Volunteer participation should remain voluntary and bounded; avoid turning saved time into ever-growing expectations.

**Capacity-adjusted role design:** Define minimum essential responsibilities separately from optional contributions; offer time-bounded roles, rotating assignments, asynchronous review, explicit backups, easy handoffs, workload transparency with privacy protection, and opt-out without shame. Board offices with statutory or fiduciary obligations cannot simply be fragmented without checking governing rules and applicable law.

**Pilot candidate:** In a willing association board, compare a baseline period with a carefully bounded AI-supported workflow for agenda preparation, action tracking and handover. Preregister board-member reported workload, number of overdue actions, handover completeness, participation equity, decision quality, privacy incidents and volunteer retention. Use a small pilot only as feasibility evidence; no causal claims without adequate comparison and confounder handling. Obtain consent, clarify who may access confidential records, and never upload sensitive board material without appropriate governance.

**Additional research questions:**
- RQ-P7: Do AI-supported handoffs and role redesign reduce **total** board-member workload rather than transferring it to another volunteer?
- RQ-P8: Do flexible roles improve sustained participation and equitable influence without weakening accountability?
- RQ-P9: In paid work, does time saved through AI become recovery/autonomy or higher output demands?
- RQ-P10: Can people with non-linear working styles participate effectively without forcing one planning or communication style, while preserving shared clarity and dependable commitments?

**Critical guardrail:** The participant's stated capacity and choice matter more than a model-inferred productivity score. Do not infer health status from working style, schedule, message patterns or AI interaction history.

## Source and evidence plan

Candidate literatures to verify bibliographically: WHO International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF); UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; capabilities approach (Sen, Nussbaum); self-determination theory (Deci and Ryan); occupational science and meaningful occupation; supported employment and individual placement/support; chronic illness and fluctuating disability; fatigue/pacing research; participatory design and disability justice; volunteer wellbeing (including selection bias); AI accessibility and automation deskilling. No claim of empirical support is made here until individual sources are checked.

The canonical Need, Value, Mission, Stakeholder, Constraint, Outcome, Evidence and Learning concepts suffice for initial experiments.
