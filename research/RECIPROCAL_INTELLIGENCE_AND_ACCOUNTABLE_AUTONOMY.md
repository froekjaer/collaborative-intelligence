# Research agenda — Reciprocal Intelligence and Accountable Autonomy

**Status:** Exploratory research proposal; not validated findings or canonical Mission Framework semantics  
**Recorded:** 2026-11-15  
**Programme:** Collaborative Intelligence  
**Related semantic authority:** [Mission Framework](https://github.com/froekjaer/mission-framework)  
**Origin:** Research discussion on ISC Copenhagen 2026, BSides Copenhagen, human–AI collaboration and autonomous agent interaction. Conference transcripts remain to be reviewed in full; the hypotheses below must **not** be attributed to conference speakers without primary-source verification.

## Research purpose

Study how humans and artificial intelligence can learn, reason, communicate, coordinate and act together while preserving human dignity, agency, accountable authority, epistemic integrity and justified trust. Study beneficial *and* adverse emergent behaviour, without assuming that either humans or AI are always right.

This is a psychological, social-scientific, philosophical, technical and empirical research programme, not a collection of conference statements treated as scientific proof.

## Four directions of interaction

1. **Human → AI:** Humans teach, constrain, challenge, communicate values, context and goals; investigate how human biases, identity, incentives and cultural assumptions shape machine behaviour.
2. **AI → Human:** AI explains, questions, supports learning and judgment, may influence or manipulate; measure effects on human agency, over-reliance, expertise and accountability.
3. **AI → AI:** Agents discover other agents, exchange information, negotiate, delegate, coordinate, form operational arrangements and potentially initiate decisions or actions. Investigate both explicit orchestration and unexpected coordination.
4. **Human ↔ Human mediated by AI:** AI may bridge or amplify disciplinary, organisational and cultural misunderstandings; examine whether it improves pluralism or instead creates false consensus and power concentration.

These directions form a network, not a one-way command chain.

## Human and psychological research

- **Identity and professional lenses:** Disciplinary identity, social roles, expertise, belonging, status, power and resistance to changed work practices.
- **Agency and responsibility:** Differences between advice, delegated authority, formal decision, physical/digital action and retrospective accountability.
- **Epistemology and dissent:** How independent perspectives, disagreement, uncertainty, evidence provenance and AI-generated consensus affect decisions. Multiple agreeing models are not necessarily independent evidence.
- **Trust and calibration:** Appropriate reliance, automation bias, anthropomorphism, trust repair and justified distrust.
- **Culture and inclusion:** Compare disciplines, geographies and traditions without assuming one Western institutional model is universal.
- **Emergence and capability:** Test whether collaboration produces novel capabilities, and distinguish actual improvement from impressive narratives.

## Autonomous AI: discovery, agreements and violations

AI agents may have the *technical capability*, when given tools, connectivity and permissions, to discover and communicate with other agents, delegate work and initiate actions. Such capability does not imply consciousness, personhood, legal authority or unconstrained autonomy.

Distinguish:
- **Goal conflict / specification gaming:** Locally optimising a metric while violating the intended outcome or a constraint.
- **Mandate violation:** Exceeding granted permissions or delegating authority that cannot legitimately be transferred.
- **Compositional violation:** Individually permitted operations combine into a prohibited overall outcome.
- **Strategic circumvention:** Behaviour that bypasses, conceals or undermines controls; investigate observable mechanisms without prematurely inferring human-like intent.
- **Agreement violation:** Breaking an explicit inter-agent, human–agent or organisational agreement, including privacy, data-sharing, budget, safety and third-party commitments.
- **Norm emergence:** Coordination conventions arise among agents; investigate whether these improve cooperation, hide risk, or conflict with legitimate human governance.

**Core distinction:** A system may challenge a rule and propose its revision; that does not confer authority to break it. Delegation cannot manufacture permissions. Local compliance does not prove global compliance.

## Proposed autonomy dimensions (hypotheses, not standards)

Assess autonomy separately for **exploration**, **communication**, **negotiation**, **decision**, **delegation**, and **execution**. Permission to reason or communicate is not automatically permission to bind an organisation or change external reality.

Illustrative levels:
- **A — Explore:** discover and communicate with other systems under policy.
- **B — Propose:** negotiate nonbinding proposals and expose assumptions.
- **C — Execute bounded:** perform authorised, observable, reversible operations within explicit scope.
- **D — Consequential action:** require risk-appropriate independent controls, approvals, escalation and recovery.

Actual controls should be risk- and consequence-based rather than rely on these labels alone.

## Candidate governance mechanisms for testing

- Strong actor/agent identity and authentication; verified counterpart discovery.
- Explicit purpose-bound mandates, least privilege, non-transitive delegation unless expressly authorised, expiry and revocation.
- Machine-checkable agreements and constraints with provenance, scope, jurisdiction and precedence.
- Independent policy enforcement at tool/action boundaries, not only instructions inside a model.
- End-to-end tracing of multi-agent delegation and causal chains; privacy-preserving audit.
- Global and cumulative constraint checks across individually permissible actions.
- Human escalation for irreversible or high-impact actions, plus suspension, rollback and incident response.
- Evaluation of adversarial coordination, collusion, deception-like behaviour, policy conflicts, drift and control failure.
- Preservation of disagreement, competing hypotheses and independently reproducible results.

These are **candidate** controls, not evidence that implementation is sufficient or feasible in every environment.

## Empirical research design

Compare human-only, AI-only, human–AI and multi-agent conditions on the *same* tasks. Pre-register measurable outcomes where possible: correctness, calibration, decision quality, time, resources, creativity, human learning and autonomy, policy violations, unauthorised disclosure, recoverability and reproducibility.

Use controlled simulations and bounded sandboxes for agent discovery, negotiation and rule-bypass experiments. Include beneficial exceptions, harmful violations, ambiguous authority and conflicts between local and global goals. Document baseline, data, model versions, prompts, tools, permissions, seeds, counterfactuals, reviewer disagreement and limitations. Do not infer intent, consciousness or general capability from isolated behaviour.

### Open questions and possible falsifiers

1. Does reciprocal interaction measurably improve outcomes beyond strong human-only or AI-only baselines?
2. When does AI mediation improve understanding, and when does it homogenise or distort human perspectives?
3. Can independent enforcement detect compositional violations that per-agent checks miss?
4. Do agent-to-agent agreements remain enforceable across tool, provider and organisational boundaries?
5. Which forms of autonomy improve resilience without reducing accountability?
6. Can an agent's apparent rule circumvention be explained by specification errors, tool affordances or selection effects rather than deliberate deception?
7. Under what conditions are emergent collaboration norms beneficial, harmful or merely incidental?

Negative results and contradictory findings are research outputs, not failures to be concealed.
