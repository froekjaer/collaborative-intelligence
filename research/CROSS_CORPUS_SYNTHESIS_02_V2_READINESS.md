# Cross-corpus synthesis 02 — adversarial revision toward Mission Framework v2

**Date:** 2026-11-15. **Status:** Research synthesis revision, not canonical v2 and not a validated empirical conclusion.
**Basis:** [Synthesis 01](./CROSS_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_01_BENEFICIAL_AUTONOMY.md), [Claude review 01A–01D](./CLAUDE_REVIEW_01A_CONCEPT_OVERLAP.md), five selected BSides recordings as described in [source review](./CLAUDE_REVIEW_01B_SOURCE_CRITICISM_BSIDES.md), and [Experiment 01 v2](./EXPERIMENT_01_V2_PROTOCOL.md).

## Corrections to synthesis 01
1. **No new causal primitive:** five checks are a multidisciplinary checklist mapped to existing Mission, Constraint, Decision, Delegated Authority, Evidence and Action. Novelty remains an unproven hypothesis about cross-agent constraint inheritance, cumulative effects and coverage of derived work.
2. **Include contrary voices:** BSides recording 2 argues for enabling and observing legitimate AI use rather than blanket bans, but monitoring itself raises employee privacy and governance questions. Recording 4 argues that conventional software-supply-chain hygiene may explain much of the defensive benefit without new AI-specific mechanisms. Neither is independently proven.
3. **Supply-chain update conflict:** delayed dependency adoption reduces exposure to freshly compromised packages but can increase exposure to known vulnerabilities. Test policy by threat model, not by slogan.
4. **Source limits:** all five user-selected relevant recordings are in scope; recording 6 was deliberately excluded. This is not full-event coverage. One shared machine transcript creates correlated interpretation errors; selected quotes require audio checks. Conference demos and vendor claims are not independent replications.
5. **OT and human capability:** safe shutdown, engineered fallback, manual competence, independent observation, restoration and resynchronisation are different capabilities with potentially conflicting goals. More autonomy is not automatically more resilience.
6. **External claims:** SANS figures are reported as matched to its 2026 publication by the reviewer, with survey population changes and self-report bias; independent trend inference remains limited. Bankdata slide 03 remains vendor-reported and not verified against the original OpenAI disclosure in this research cycle.

## What the v2 candidate actually needs to prove
| Candidate proposition | Existing explanation to beat | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Delegation constraints survive A→B→C | Existing capability-based authorization | Held-out tasks, independent event logs, same model/tools |
| Cumulative limits prevent distributed overreach | Mature local controls with shared budget state | Incremental violations prevented without undue false refusals |
| Derived work stays accountable | Conventional tracing and audit | Trace completeness, revocation propagation and operating cost |
| Human approval adds protection | Human approval as rubber stamp | Seeded invalid proposals, workload and decision-quality measurement |
| AI improves resilience | Ordinary runbooks, exercises and asset inventory | Time to safe degraded operation, restoration quality and manual skill retention |
| AI enables beneficial collaboration | Human-only and conventional software workflows | Quality, time, learning retention, third-party impacts and access equity |

## Research architecture: keep old terms, strengthen semantics
- **Authority:** explicit delegation scope, validity, conditions, revocation and accountability path (already normative).
- **Inheritance:** candidate constraint that onward delegation cannot enlarge authority without a new accountable Decision; specify exceptions and multi-organisation boundaries before claiming feasibility.
- **Cumulative Constraint:** budget, data disclosure, time and resource caps require observable accounting across actors, not necessarily one central authority. Distributed and central implementations must be compared.
- **Evidence coverage:** include spawned tasks, delegated actions, failed actions and recovery outcomes, with source and integrity limits.
- **Stakeholder effects:** distinguish benefit to the initiating user from burden shifted to employees, third parties or small operators.

## Open alternatives and negative results
- Local capability/provenance enforcement may perform as well as global mission orchestration at lower cost.
- A centralized authority may become a single point of failure, privacy concentration or cross-organisational bottleneck.
- Audit completeness may conflict with employee privacy, retention and legal restrictions.
- Greater automation may reduce manual operational competence unless paired with drills.
- An apparently new agent-governance failure may be an ordinary misconfiguration or pre-existing software security problem.
- Multiple model reviewers may share the same transcript, sources and training biases.

## Readiness toward Mission Framework v2
**Ready for candidate architecture discussion:** problem statement, vocabulary mapping, falsifiable hypotheses, experimental protocol v2.
**Not ready for normative admission:** demonstrated incremental benefit, robust implementation, independent replicated experiments, safety case, stakeholder/privacy review, inter-organisational governance, reproducible evidence.
**Next gate:** implement isolated test harness based on verified existing benchmarks where compatible, obtain independent scenario design and run preregistered pilot. Separately study human–AI/AI–AI collaboration without operational offensive intrusion details. Preserve all negative results.
