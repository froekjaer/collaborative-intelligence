# Collaborative Intelligence — v2 research foundations

**Status:** Conceptual research candidate, not canonical or empirically validated. 2026-11-15.  
**Authority:** Collaborative Intelligence research programme. Canonical Mission Framework vocabulary remains in [GLOSSARY](https://github.com/froekjaer/mission-framework/blob/main/GLOSSARY.md).  
**Predecessor:** [README](../README.md), [cross-corpus synthesis 02](./CROSS_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_02_V2_READINESS.md), [Experiment 01 v2](./EXPERIMENT_01_V2_PROTOCOL.md). **Related unmerged research:** PR #12 and draft PR #13; do not silently incorporate their findings as accepted.

## Research proposition

> Under what conditions can human beings, AI systems and organised collectives combine complementary capabilities to create better-justified knowledge, decisions and beneficial changes in reality, while preserving human agency, equitable access, diversity, learning, resource sustainability and accountability for consequences to people and the non-human world?

This **extends** the repository's original research question; it does not replace or claim to prove it. Collaborative Intelligence is a research proposition, not a declaration that more participants or AI necessarily improve decisions.

## Distinctions that must not collapse

1. **Human cognition and agency**: lived experience, embodied skills, moral judgement, motivation and accountability.
2. **Artificial intelligence**: computational inference and tool-mediated action; model fluency is not justified knowledge or moral authority.
3. **Collective/social intelligence**: institutions, cultures, practices, expertise, knowledge transmission, power and coordination.
4. **Environmental and material feedback**: people, animals, ecosystems, machines, physical infrastructure and externalities are reality, not automatically 'intelligent agents'. They supply observations, constraints and consequences.

**Religions, secular traditions and philosophies** are sources of lived meaning, institutions, moral reasoning and contested normative claims; they must be studied both internally and critically, not flattened into interchangeable value labels.

## Six value questions (research dimensions, not new canonical primitives)

| Dimension | Question | Counter-risk / disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Needs and distributive justice | Do those with justified needs receive appropriate benefits, and who decides priorities when resources are scarce? | Elite capture, paternalism, exclusion, displacement of unmet needs |
| Learning and intergenerational transfer | Do younger people develop independent competence and can elders transfer knowledge in reciprocal learning? | Deskilling, token mentorship, age stereotypes |
| Diversity and epistemic inclusion | Does meaningful participation across gender, age, disability, neurodiversity, class, cultures, geography and disciplines improve deliberation? | Representation without influence, forced consensus, privacy harms |
| Sustainable resources | Are human workload, wellbeing, recovery, AI energy/compute and environmental resources protected? | Work intensification, surveillance, rebound effects, hallucination loops |
| Innovation and organisational development | Do capabilities create genuinely useful innovations and more resilient organisations? | Output volume mistaken for value, centralisation, lock-in |
| Freedom and agency | Does efficiency increase substantive freedom, self-determination and meaningful time? | Productivity gains appropriated by one actor, dependence, loss of choice |

**Do not combine** all dimensions into a single unexamined score. Rights, safety and ecological constraints may be non-compensatory. Stakeholder trade-offs and uncertainty remain visible.

## Reality Belt: bidirectional causal and epistemic discipline

The existing canonical Mission Loop runs Reality → Need → Mission → ... → Outcome → Learning → Reality. The proposed research lens asks for a traceable **two-way** connection between:

- needs, culture, ethics and authority;
- people / processes / technology, with institutions and non-human impacts explicitly considered;
- physical and social reality, including people, animals, ecosystems, infrastructure and long-term consequences.

**Outbound:** decision → action → observed outcomes, including unintended harms and distributional effects.  
**Inbound:** observations, affected stakeholders' testimony, uncertainty and contradictions → challenge of claims, revision of decisions, policies and sometimes mission objectives.

Distinguish intended outputs, observed outcomes and **causally attributable** effects. A process can report success while people, animals or ecosystems fare worse. A plausible story is not evidence of causality; compare appropriate counterfactuals where feasible.

**Neuroinclusion and youth capability development:** The diversity and learning dimensions explicitly include young people with diagnoses, neurodivergent young people and differing functional profiles. Research individual strengths and support needs, accessible opportunities, reciprocal mentorship, voluntary participation and two-way adaptation of tasks/environments. Do not infer talents from diagnoses or equate employment with dignity. See [Neuroinclusion and youth capabilities — research track 01](./NEUROINCLUSION_YOUTH_CAPABILITIES_01.md). This is a candidate research programme, not an established intervention.

## Research programme: five workstreams

**W1 — Human, cultural and civilisational foundations.** Evolution without teleology; cultural evolution, anthropology, religious studies, historical and contemporary philosophy, gender and intersectionality, Indigenous knowledge governance. Study competing perspectives and translations with scholarly review. Source seed: interdisciplinary source register on PR #13 (not merged).

**W2 — Collaboration mechanisms.** Specify coordination, complementarity, disagreement, expertise selection, knowledge provenance, contestability, delegation and authority. Compare human-only, human–human, human–AI and AI–AI conditions; avoid treating AI–AI interaction as equivalent to human relationships.

**W3 — Reality and stakeholder consequences.** Map causal chains and material effects for all affected stakeholders, including non-human interests represented through legitimate human and institutional processes. Test distribution of benefit, externalities, ecological burden and correction loops.

**W4 — Learning, wellbeing and freedom.** Study mentorship across generations, retention of unassisted skills, human autonomy, meaningful participation, gender and cultural inclusion, burnout and rebound effects of automation.

**W5 — Governance, engineering and reproducibility.** Reuse canonical Mission Framework terms and OP-001, isolate synthetic prototypes, compare mature conventional baselines, publish negative results and explicit evidence grades. Avoid normative changes before independent challenge.

## First falsifiable research questions

- **RQ1 (complementarity):** Does human–AI collaboration outperform strong human-only and conventional-tool conditions on *both* task quality and calibration, controlling for time, skill and cost?
- **RQ2 (epistemic diversity):** Does structured dissent from diverse expertise improve detection of seeded errors without disproportionately increasing delay or exclusion?
- **RQ3 (learning):** Does AI-assisted mentorship improve delayed unassisted performance for younger participants compared with conventional mentorship, and does reciprocal mentoring benefit older participants?
- **RQ4 (stakeholder distribution):** Who gains time, money, safety and agency, and who bears new burdens? Compare outcomes across gender, age, disability and other relevant groups with consent and privacy protection.
- **RQ5 (Reality Belt):** Does explicit physical/social feedback and externality tracking reduce false-success declarations relative to process-only metrics?
- **RQ6 (resource sustainability):** Do added AI agents improve correct task completion per total human/compute/environmental resource, or mainly add cost, errors and review work?
- **RQ7 (freedom):** Does efficiency translate into demonstrable participant autonomy and usable free time rather than only higher output demands?
- **RQ8 (governance):** Do mission-wide constraints add value beyond established capability, provenance and cumulative controls? See Experiment 01 v2; a negative answer must be publishable.

## Initial evidence and evaluation contract

For every research claim record: stable ID, source, discipline/tradition, date, source status, relevant population and geography, method, uncertainty, counterevidence, causal inference limits, stakeholder impacts and proposed test. For normative claims distinguish **value commitment** from **empirical proposition**.

Minimum pilot pattern: preregistered task/scenario definitions; independently graded outcomes; strong comparators; qualitative accounts of affected participants; delayed competence measurement where relevant; ecological and human resource accounting; distributional analysis; explicit stopping and ethics review for human participants. Never infer that a test fixture proves real-world benefits.

## Governance and architecture boundaries

- **Collaborative Intelligence** owns research questions, competing theories, methods, evidence and cross-disciplinary synthesis.
- **Mission Framework** owns normative semantics and governance requirements. No duplicate glossary or silent redefinition here.
- **Mission Platform** and reference missions operationalise and challenge claims, without independently changing canonical meaning.
- **Publication Pipeline** publishes reviewed, provenance-preserving results; it does not become an authority for truth.
- All findings from implementations that generalise upstream require the Framework Findings / ADR path.

**Open cross-repository propagation gap:** the value-and-reality research questions may eventually justify canonical glossary/Framework Findings updates; no change is proposed before evidence and architectural review. Track this in this research document and PR; assess formally before any normative integration.

## Explicit unknowns

Whether Collaborative Intelligence is a distinct explanatory theory, an integration framework, a design method or simply a research programme is **not yet settled**. Its originality, generalisability, effect sizes, cultural portability and institutional feasibility are unproven. Those are research questions, not marketing claims.
